Recent methods for photorealistic avatar creation have shown remarkable progress in synthesizing high-fidelity human images from either static 3D scans or monocular / sparse-multi-view videos. However, they primarily fall into two categories: person-agnostic methods can quickly build static 3D avatars but produce animations that lack realistic surface dynamics such as clothing wrinkles, while person-specific approaches can achieve better realism but require expensive multi-view captures for each individual. To bridge this gap, we introduce AvatarDynamizer, a generative method that transforms an off-the-shelf static 3D avatar into a controllable, realistic, and multi-view-consistent 4D avatar. Our key idea is to formulate the problem of avatar dynamics modeling as a conditional texture generation task, leveraging a novel texture-space surface-dynamics embedding that encodes pose-dependent dynamics into dynamic texture maps. This design allows us to build upon and leverage the generative power of existing video diffusion models, while ultimately decoding the generated dynamic textures into 3D Gaussians for multi-view consistent rendering. To facilitate training, we further compile a new large-scale multi-view dataset with long sequences that captures diverse motions and dynamics. Experiments show that our method can animate a wide range of static avatars with convincing dynamics and outperforms other generalizable baselines in visual quality, especially under limited dynamic training data.
